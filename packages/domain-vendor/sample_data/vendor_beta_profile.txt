DataStream Analytics is a data platform vendor founded in 2017 in Denver, Colorado. The
product is a managed analytics stack that combines streaming ingestion, a columnar warehouse,
and an ML pipeline service. Target buyers are data engineering teams at mid-market SaaS
companies and digital-native enterprises that have outgrown a single warehouse and need
low-latency operational analytics. The control plane is multi-tenant; customer data planes
can run in DataStream-hosted VPCs or in the customer’s AWS/Azure account (bring-your-own-cloud).

Capabilities. Real-time analytics: Kafka-compatible ingestion, 5-second dashboard freshness
on standard workloads, and materialized views with incremental refresh. Data warehousing:
separated storage and compute, ANSI SQL, time-travel for 30 days on Professional and 90 days
on Enterprise, and native connectors for Salesforce, Snowflake export, and S3. ML pipeline:
feature store, scheduled training jobs on CPU/GPU, and batch plus streaming inference
endpoints. Governance includes column-level access control, audit logs retained for 365 days,
and PII detection on ingest. The company holds SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001; HIPAA BAA is
available on Enterprise.

Pricing is usage-based with compute-hour billing. Compute is $0.42 per compute-hour for
standard warehouses and $1.15 per GPU-hour for training jobs. Storage is $24 per TB-month
for hot data and $8 per TB-month for infrequent access. Streaming ingest is $0.10 per GB.
A monthly platform fee of $1,500 (Professional) or $6,000 (Enterprise) covers control plane,
support, and connector usage. Example: a customer running 4,000 compute-hours, 20 TB hot
storage, and 2 TB ingest would see roughly $1,680 compute + $480 storage + $200 ingest +
$1,500 platform ≈ $3,860 per month before committed-use discounts. Annual committed spend
of $50,000 unlocks 18% off compute. Overage above committed hours bills at list price.

DataStream positions as the “streaming-first warehouse” against Snowflake, Databricks, and
warehouse-plus-Kafka assemblies. Differentiators claimed: sub-10-second freshness without a
separate serving layer, BYOC data-plane option, and a single bill for stream + warehouse +
training. Risks: usage-based bills can spike during backfills; GPU queues exist in us-east;
there is no on-premises appliance; and the ML feature store is younger than specialist ML
platforms. Integration is strongest with AWS; Azure BYOC is generally available, GCP is
preview.
